## How this calculator works

This tool uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation — the formula with the best
research support for estimating resting metabolism — then multiplies by a
standard activity factor. Every mainstream calculator does roughly this. The
difference is what we do with the output: instead of pretending the result is
exact, we show the ±10% band the formula actually earns.

## Why a range beats a single number

Two people with identical stats can burn hundreds of calories apart per day.
Muscle mass, fidgeting, job activity, and even how accurately you log food all
move your true maintenance. A single number invites false confidence; a range
invites the right habit — start inside it, watch your two-week trend, and
adjust in small steps.

## What to do with your number

Pick the middle of your range and eat there for 14 days while logging
honestly. If your weight trends the way the table above predicts, the estimate
was good. If not, adjust by 100–150 kcal and repeat. That feedback loop — not
the calculator — is what actually finds your TDEE.
