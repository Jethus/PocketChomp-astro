## How this protein calculator works

Instead of taking a flat percentage of calories, this calculator derives your daily protein range from **body weight (g/kg)**, adjusted for your goal and how often you train:

- **Health Canada RDA floor (0.8 g/kg):** the minimum reference intake to prevent deficiency in sedentary adults — shown in the results card as a baseline, not an optimal target for active people.
- **Everyday active maintenance (1.2–1.6 g/kg):** aligned with the joint Dietitians of Canada, Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, and ACSM position for active adults.
- **Muscle gain or fat loss while training (1.6–2.2 g/kg):** meta-analyses (including Morton et al., *BJSM* 2018) show lean-mass gains plateau around 1.6 g/kg/day, with intakes up to 2.0–2.2 g/kg useful during short-term fat loss in resistance-trained adults to protect muscle and manage hunger.

## What 30–40 grams of protein looks like in Canadian groceries

Numbers on a screen are easier to use once you picture real food. Roughly 30–40 g of protein is:

- 130–160 g cooked chicken breast or lean ground turkey
- 300–350 g plain 0% Greek yogurt (such as Oikos or Kirkland Signature) or 2% cottage cheese
- 150 g cooked salmon or tuna, or 250–300 g extra-firm tofu
- One scoop of whey or plant protein powder (~25 g) mixed with 250 mL Fairlife or high-protein lactose-free milk (~14–18 g)
